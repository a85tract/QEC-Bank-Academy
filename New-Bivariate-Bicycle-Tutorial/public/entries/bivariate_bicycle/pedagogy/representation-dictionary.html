Representation dictionary

BB codes are unusually easy to misname because the same matrices sit inside several broader constructions. This dictionary records what each view makes transparent, which data it preserves, and which inference it does not license.

Polynomial to group algebra

The monomial \(x^a y^b\) labels the group element \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z_l\times\mathbb Z_m\). Acting by the regular representation turns group multiplication into matrix multiplication. Since the group is abelian, the regular matrices for \(A\) and \(B\) commute.

This translation explains the relation row:

\[ \text{BB}\subset\text{abelian 2BGA}\subset\text{two-block codes}. \]

wang2023abelian develops the abelian/nonabelian two-block setting, while lin2023twoblock places 2BGA codes inside lifted products. The containments are not equalities:

  • a 2BGA code may use another group;
  • a nonabelian construction can make left and right actions commute without a two-variable abelian polynomial ring;
  • a general lifted product can have larger base matrices than the two-block BB complex.

Group algebra to chain complex

The maps \([B;A]\) and \([A|B]\) are \(R\)-linear. Their composition vanishes, so they define a length-two complex. Forgetting \(R\)-linearity gives the binary CSS matrices; retaining it exposes translations and quotient modules.

The two descriptions preserve the exact code when the same bases and regular representation are fixed. Changing bases can preserve a code space while changing row/qubit order; that distinction matters for the Atlas replay contract, which requires exact matrices rather than equivalence.

Chain complex to quotient module

The image of \([A|B]\) is the ideal \((A,B)\). The quotient \(R/(A,B)\) therefore measures one rank defect. Factorization and common-root methods can predict when this quotient is nonzero and how its dimension changes with periods or boundaries. postema2025existence uses this structure to characterize existence and asymptotic limitations; wang2026bbs extends the root/boundary analysis.

Rank is still not distance. A quotient class can have a low-weight representative, or every representative may be spread across the torus. Distance asks for the lightest nontrivial class after all stabilizer reductions.

Polynomial to Tanner and circuit views

Every nonzero entry of \(A\) or \(B\) is an edge between one check and one data qubit. The gross three-plus-three terms give weight-six checks and three X plus three Z incidences per data qubit. bravyi2024bicycle shows how this graph splits into two planar degree-three coupling layers.

The graph is necessary but insufficient for a fault circuit. A Tanner edge says two systems interact; it does not specify:

  • which ancilla owns a check;
  • when the interaction occurs;
  • control/target orientation;
  • reset and measurement timing;
  • which measurement products are detectors.

shaw2025morphing changes the instantaneous code and graph, illustrating why connectivity reduction cannot be described as a mere redrawing of the same static matrix.

Quotients to anyons

chen2025anyon interprets polynomial/Koszul data as excitation types, mobility constraints, and topological frustration. This picture asks which defects can move under local operators and how global torus constraints create logical sectors.

The caution is essential: the existence of \(k/2\) quotient dimensions does not mean the Hamiltonian is simply \(k/2\) decoupled toric codes. Logical degeneracy, local-Clifford phase equivalence, and decoder decomposition are different equivalence relations.

Anyons to toric-cylinder decoding

sahay2026matching exploits symmetries to expose toric-like cylinders and apply MWPM, then combines the result with BP and over-matching. The preserved object is the selected decoding problem: syndromes and logical ambiguity under the reduction.

This does not contradict topological frustration. A decoder may map parts of an inference problem to familiar cylinders without producing a finite-depth local unitary that decomposes the entire BB Hamiltonian into toric copies.

Choosing the right view

Use:

  • polynomials to construct;
  • group algebra to classify the parent relation;
  • the chain complex to reason about logical quotients;
  • quotient modules/roots to predict rank and boundary effects;
  • Tanner graphs to design couplers;
  • explicit circuits/detectors to discuss thresholds;
  • anyons to classify excitations;
  • cylinders to justify a matching decoder.

Calling all of them “the BB representation” erases the question each translation was built to answer.
