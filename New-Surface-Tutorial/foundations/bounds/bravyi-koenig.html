The Bravyi–König bound for topological Pauli stabilizer codes

Level 2 derivation: why a geometrically local, bounded-spread logical unitary on a \(D\)-dimensional topological Pauli stabilizer code cannot rise above level \(D\) of the Clifford hierarchy.

The memorable slogan is:

A constant-depth local circuit gets one hierarchy level per spatial dimension.

The proof is not a statement about the surface code's particular square lattice. It uses four ingredients that survive every change of stabilizer layout:

  1. local circuits have bounded light cones;
  2. logical Paulis can be cleaned out of correctable regions;
  3. a \(D\)-dimensional lattice can be partitioned into \(D+1\) correctable regions;
  4. each group commutator removes one region from the possible support.

After \(D\) commutators, the remaining operator is trapped in the last correctable region and therefore acts as a phase on the codespace. Reading that statement backward is exactly the recursive definition of the Clifford hierarchy.

1. Precise statement

Put physical qubits on a \(D\)-dimensional lattice. Let a Pauli stabilizer code have

  • stabilizer generators of geometric diameter at most \(\xi\);
  • distance \(d\);
  • a unitary \(U\), built from a depth-\(h\) circuit whose gates have geometric range at most \(r\);
  • and the property that \(U\) preserves the codespace.

Write \(\overline U\) for the induced logical unitary. If \(D\geq 2\) and the length scales obey

\[ \xi,\;hr \ll d^{1/D}, \]

then

\[ \overline U\in\mathcal C_D, \]

where \(\mathcal C_D\) is level \(D\) of the Clifford hierarchy.

For a family with bounded \(\xi,h,r\) and growing distance, the scale inequality holds for every sufficiently large member. “Constant depth” is therefore the clean asymptotic formulation, but the actual resource in the proof is the light-cone radius \(hr\).

The paper proves a stronger morphism version. The source and target may be two different \(D\)-dimensional topological Pauli stabilizer codes on the same lattice, with the same number of logical qubits. If \(U\) maps the first codespace unitarily onto the second, use the smaller distance and the larger stabilizer range in the scale inequality; the induced map is still in \(\mathcal C_D\). The codespace-preserving theorem is the special case in which the two codes coincide.

Immediate specializations:

  • \(D=2\): \(\overline U\in\mathcal C_2\), the Clifford group. A logical \(T\) gate is excluded.
  • \(D=3\): \(\overline U\in\mathcal C_3\). This level contains non-Clifford gates such as \(T\), so the theorem permits them but does not guarantee that a given code has one.

For the modern convention \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/4})\), \(TXT^\dagger=(X+Y)/\sqrt2\), which is a Clifford but not a Pauli, while \(TZT^\dagger=Z\). Thus \(T\in\mathcal C_3\setminus\mathcal C_2\), making it the simplest witness to the difference between the two- and three-dimensional ceilings.

2. Dictionary

All Pauli phases are irrelevant below.

2.1 Stabilizers, logicals, and encoded action

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the stabilizer group and \(\Pi\) the codespace projector. A physical Pauli \(P\) is a logical Pauli when it commutes with every stabilizer. Two such Paulis have the same encoded action when they differ by a stabilizer.

Choose an encoding isometry

\[ J:(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes k}\longrightarrow (\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}, \qquad JJ^\dagger=\Pi . \]

For a physical operator \(O\) preserving the codespace, its encoded action is

\[ \overline O=J^\dagger OJ . \]

The distance \(d\) is the minimum weight of a logical Pauli whose encoded action is not a scalar.

The map from commuting physical Paulis to encoded Paulis has kernel exactly \(\mathcal S\), up to phase. Suppose a Pauli \(P\) commutes with \(\mathcal S\), fixes every code state, and is independent of \(\mathcal S\). Adding \(P\) as a new independent stabilizer would halve the joint \(+1\) eigenspace, yet by assumption it would leave that eigenspace unchanged. The contradiction shows that \(P\) was already a stabilizer.

2.2 Correctable regions

A set of qubits \(M\) is correctable when every logical Pauli supported entirely in \(M\) acts as a scalar on the codespace. Any set with fewer than \(d\) qubits is correctable by the definition of distance.

Correctability is stronger than the Pauli-only wording suggests. If \(O\) is any operator supported in \(M\), expand it in the Pauli basis:

\[ O=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha P_\alpha . \]

For each \(P_\alpha\):

  • if it anticommutes with a stabilizer \(S\), then \(\Pi P_\alpha\Pi=\Pi SP_\alpha\Pi =-\Pi P_\alpha S\Pi=-\Pi P_\alpha\Pi=0\);
  • if it commutes with every stabilizer, it is a logical Pauli in \(M\), so correctability gives \(\Pi P_\alpha\Pi=x_\alpha\Pi\).

Therefore

\[ \Pi O\Pi=c(O)\Pi \tag{2.1} \]

for every operator supported in a correctable region. In particular, a logical unitary supported there is logically only a phase.

2.3 Neighborhoods and circuit range

For a region \(M\), let \(B_\rho(M)\) contain all lattice sites within distance \(\rho\) of \(M\). A depth-\(h\), range-\(r\) circuit expands support by at most \(hr\):

\[ \operatorname{supp}(UOU^\dagger) \subseteq B_{hr}(\operatorname{supp}O). \tag{2.2} \]

This follows one layer at a time. In one layer, only gates meeting the current support can matter, and each such gate reaches at most distance \(r\).

2.4 Clifford hierarchy

Let \(\mathcal C_1\) be the logical Pauli group. Recursively,

\[ \mathcal C_j= \{V:VPV^\dagger\in\mathcal C_{j-1} \text{ for every logical Pauli }P\}. \tag{2.3} \]

Thus \(\mathcal C_2\) is the Clifford group. The hierarchy is nested, but \(\mathcal C_j\) is generally not a group for \(j\geq3\). Multiplying an element of \(\mathcal C_j\) on either side by a Pauli leaves it in \(\mathcal C_j\); this follows directly from (2.3), inducting on \(j\).

For unitaries \(A,B\), write their group commutator as

\[ [A,B]=ABA^\dagger B^\dagger . \]

The proof repeatedly asks what happens to the hierarchy level when such a commutator becomes simpler.

3. Four lemmas

3.1 Cleaning Lemma

Lemma. If \(M\) is correctable, every logical Pauli \(P\) has an equivalent representative with no support in \(M\).

Proof. Represent phase-free \(n\)-qubit Paulis by vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^{2n}\), with the symplectic product recording commutation. Let \(\mathsf S\) be the vector space spanned by the stabilizers, let \(\mathsf S^\perp\) be its symplectic orthogonal, and let \(\pi_M\) restrict a Pauli vector to \(M\).

A logical Pauli is a vector \(p\in\mathsf S^\perp\). We need to find \(s\in\mathsf S\) with

\[ \pi_M(p+s)=0, \]

or equivalently \(\pi_M(p)\in\pi_M(\mathsf S)\).

Assume instead that \(\pi_M(p)\notin\pi_M(\mathsf S)\). The symplectic form on the Pauli space of \(M\) is nondegenerate, so there is a Pauli vector \(q\) supported in \(M\) such that

\[ \langle q,\pi_M(s)\rangle=0 \quad\text{for every }s\in\mathsf S, \qquad \langle q,\pi_M(p)\rangle=1 . \]

Extended by zero outside \(M\), \(q\) commutes with every stabilizer and is therefore a logical Pauli supported in \(M\). Correctability says its encoded action is scalar, so \(q\) is a stabilizer up to phase. But \(p\), being logical, must commute with every stabilizer, contradicting \(\langle q,p\rangle=1\). Hence the required \(s\) exists. Multiplying \(P\) by its stabilizer representative removes its support from \(M\) without changing its encoded action. \(\square\)

Cleaning does not erase a logical operator. It moves the same logical class out of a region known to contain no logical information.

3.2 Union Lemma

Lemma. Let \(M\) and \(N\) be correctable regions separated by more than the maximum stabilizer-generator diameter \(\xi\). Then \(M\cup N\) is correctable.

Proof. Let a logical Pauli \(P\) be supported in \(M\cup N\), and factor it as \(P=P_M P_N\). No stabilizer generator can meet both regions. For a generator meeting only \(M\), commutation with \(P\) is exactly commutation with \(P_M\); similarly for \(N\). Because \(P\) commutes with every generator, both \(P_M\) and \(P_N\) commute with every generator separately. They are logical Paulis supported in correctable regions, so both have scalar encoded action. Their product does too. \(\square\)

Induction gives the version used below: a union of mutually well-separated correctable chunks is correctable. This is the first indispensable use of stabilizer locality. Without local generators, one generator could touch two distant chunks and the factorization argument would fail.

3.3 Causal-intersection Lemma

Lemma. Let \(P,Q\) be tensor-product Paulis and let \(U\) be a depth-\(h\), range-\(r\) circuit. Up to a constant factor in the radius, the commutator

\[ K=[P,UQU^\dagger] \]

can have support only where the causal neighborhoods of \(\operatorname{supp}P\) and \(\operatorname{supp}Q\) meet.

Proof. Draw the circuit for \(UQU^\dagger\). Delete every gate of \(U\) and \(U^\dagger\) outside the forward and backward light cones of \(\operatorname{supp}Q\). Those gates occur in mutually inverse pairs and cancel, so this deletion does not change \(UQU^\dagger\).

Now place this pruned circuit next to its inverse in \(P(UQU^\dagger)P^\dagger(UQ^\dagger U^\dagger)\). Delete every remaining gate whose causal cone misses \(\operatorname{supp}P\). It also meets its inverse without encountering \(P\), so it cancels. What survives must lie both near \(\operatorname{supp}Q\) and near \(\operatorname{supp}P\). The composite circuit has depth \(O(h)\), so the thickening radius is \(O(hr)\). \(\square\)

This is the second indispensable use of locality. A long-range gate can join the two supports without passing through their geometric intersection.

3.4 Pauli-character Lemma

Lemma. Let \(V\) be a logical unitary. If

\[ [P,V]\propto I \]

for every logical Pauli \(P\), then \(V\) is a logical Pauli up to phase.

Proof. The assumption is equivalent to

\[ PVP^\dagger=\chi(P)V . \]

Conjugating twice by the same Pauli gives \(\chi(P)^2=1\), so every \(\chi(P)\) is a commutation sign. Expand \(V\) in the logical Pauli basis. Every nonzero Pauli component must have the same commutation sign \(\chi(P)\) against every \(P\). Two distinct Paulis cannot have identical commutation signs against the entire Pauli group: their product would be a nonidentity Pauli commuting with every Pauli, contradicting nondegeneracy of the symplectic form. Only one Pauli coefficient can therefore be nonzero. \(\square\)

4. The geometric partition

Choose a mesoscopic length \(R\) satisfying

\[ \max(\xi,c_Dhr)\ll R\ll d_*^{1/D}, \tag{4.1} \]

where \(d_*\) is the smaller source/target distance and \(c_D\) is a constant large enough to contain every light cone created by the \(D\) nested commutators below.

Triangulate the physical lattice at scale \(R\). Thicken the simplices in order of dimension:

  • \(\Lambda_1\) is a union of separated neighborhoods of vertices;
  • \(\Lambda_2\) is a union of separated interiors of edges after the vertex neighborhoods are removed;
  • continue through faces and higher-dimensional simplices;
  • \(\Lambda_{D+1}\) is the union of the remaining interiors of the \(D\)-simplices.

These \(D+1\) regions partition the lattice. Each \(\Lambda_j\) is a union of chunks with diameter \(O(R)\), while distinct chunks in the same region are separated by \(\Omega(R)\).

A fixed-radius neighborhood of one chunk contains \(O(R^D)\) qubits. The right inequality in (4.1) makes this number smaller than \(d_*\), so the chunk and every light-cone neighborhood needed below are correctable for both codes. The left inequality keeps distinct thickened chunks farther apart than the stabilizer range. The Union Lemma then makes each complete \(\Lambda_j\), with the required collars, correctable.

The two-dimensional picture

For \(D=2\), triangulate the surface into triangles of side \(R\):

  • \(C\): disks around the vertices;
  • \(B\): thickened edge segments after removing those disks;
  • \(A\): the capped triangle interiors left over.

Each letter denotes many disconnected pieces, but pieces bearing the same letter are far apart. Each letter is therefore one correctable region by the Union Lemma.

On the toric or surface code, one can instead picture a horizontal logical string and a displaced vertical logical string. After conjugation by a local circuit, a string becomes a fat string; its commutator with the transverse string lives near their small intersection. This is the right intuition. It is not the general proof: an arbitrary two-dimensional stabilizer code need not present every logical as a simple string. The \(A,B,C\) partition plus cleaning supplies the same “small intersection” conclusion without assuming string-shaped logicals.

5. Complete proof in two dimensions

Use the stronger two-code formulation. Let \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\) project onto the source and target codespaces, and suppose

\[ U\Pi_1U^\dagger=\Pi_2 . \]

Choose the partition \(\Lambda=A\cup B\cup C\) above, with collars wide enough for all circuit light cones.

Let \(Q\) be an arbitrary logical Pauli of the source code and \(P\) an arbitrary logical Pauli of the target code.

  1. Since \(B_\rho(A)\) is correctable for the source code, clean \(Q\) out of \(B_\rho(A)\).
  2. Since \(B_\rho(B)\) is correctable for the target code, clean \(P\) out of \(B_\rho(B)\).
  3. Form the target-code logical operator
\[ K=P(UQU^\dagger)P^\dagger(UQ^\dagger U^\dagger). \tag{5.1} \]

The cleaned \(Q\) has no support near \(A\), so the bounded light cone of \(UQU^\dagger\) avoids \(A\). The cleaned \(P\) has no support near \(B\). By the Causal-intersection Lemma, all parts of (5.1) away from the common causal intersection cancel. Thus \(K\) is supported in \(C\).

Every factor in (5.1) preserves the target codespace: \(P\) is a target logical, while \(UQU^\dagger\) is the image of a source logical under the codespace morphism. Hence \(K\) is a logical unitary supported in the correctable region \(C\). Equation (2.1) gives

\[ K\Pi_2=c\Pi_2,\qquad |c|=1. \tag{5.2} \]

In fact \(c=\pm1\). On the codespace let \(R=\overline U\,\overline Q\,\overline U^\dagger\). Equation (5.2) says

\[ \overline P R\overline P^\dagger R^\dagger=cI, \quad\text{or}\quad \overline P R\overline P^\dagger=cR . \]

Conjugating a second time by the Pauli \(\overline P\) yields \(R=c^2R\), so \(c^2=1\).

We have proved

\[ [\overline P, \overline U\,\overline Q\,\overline U^\dagger] =\pm I \tag{5.3} \]

for every target Pauli \(\overline P\) and every source Pauli \(\overline Q\). Fixing \(Q\), the Pauli-character Lemma turns (5.3) into

\[ \overline U\,\overline Q\,\overline U^\dagger \in\mathcal C_1 . \]

This is true for every \(\overline Q\), which is precisely the definition

\[ \overline U\in\mathcal C_2 . \]

So every such two-dimensional logical unitary is Clifford. No translation symmetry, square lattice, homological presentation, or string form for the logical operators entered the proof.

6. The \(D\)-dimensional induction

Partition the lattice into the \(D+1\) correctable regions

\[ \Lambda=\Lambda_1\cup\cdots\cup\Lambda_{D+1}. \]

Choose an arbitrary source logical Pauli \(P_1\), and arbitrary target logical Paulis \(P_2,\ldots,P_D\). Use the Cleaning Lemma to choose representatives satisfying

\[ \operatorname{supp}(P_j)\cap B_\rho(\Lambda_j)=\varnothing, \qquad j=1,\ldots,D. \tag{6.1} \]

Define

\[ \begin{aligned} K_1 &= UP_1U^\dagger,\\ K_j &=P_j^\dagger K_{j-1}P_jK_{j-1}^\dagger, \qquad j=2,\ldots,D . \end{aligned} \tag{6.2} \]

Every \(K_j\) preserves the target codespace. We now track support.

  • Because \(P_1\) was cleaned out of a collar of \(\Lambda_1\), causal pruning of the circuit \(UP_1U^\dagger\) gives a representation of \(K_1\) acting trivially on \(\Lambda_1\).
  • Suppose \(K_{j-1}\) acts trivially on \(\Lambda_1\cup\cdots\cup\Lambda_{j-1}\). The next line of (6.2) is its commutator with \(P_j\). Since \(P_j\) was cleaned out of a collar of \(\Lambda_j\), the Causal-intersection Lemma removes \(\Lambda_j\) from the support as well. The already removed regions stay removed because their collars were chosen wider than the new constant-depth light cone.

The depth of these nested commutator circuits grows by only a \(D\)-dependent constant factor. That is why one common radius \(\rho=c_Dhr\) suffices in (4.1). Induction gives

\[ \operatorname{supp}(K_j) \subseteq \Lambda_{j+1}\cup\cdots\cup\Lambda_{D+1}. \tag{6.3} \]

At \(j=D\), \(K_D\) is a logical unitary supported in the correctable region \(\Lambda_{D+1}\). Therefore

\[ \overline K_D\propto I \tag{6.4} \]

for every choice of \(P_1,\ldots,P_D\).

Now read the nested commutators backward.

First, (6.2) and (6.4) say that

\[ [\overline P_D,\overline K_{D-1}]\propto I \]

for every Pauli \(\overline P_D\). The Pauli-character Lemma gives

\[ \overline K_{D-1}\in\mathcal C_1 . \]

For the induction step, suppose \(\overline K_j\in\mathcal C_m\) for every choice of \(\overline P_j\). Rearranging (6.2) gives

\[ \overline K_{j-1}\overline P_j\overline K_{j-1}^\dagger =\overline P_j\,\overline K_j . \tag{6.5} \]

The right-hand side belongs to \(\mathcal C_m\), because multiplying by a Pauli preserves a hierarchy level. Since (6.5) holds for every Pauli \(\overline P_j\), definition (2.3) gives

\[ \overline K_{j-1}\in\mathcal C_{m+1}. \]

Repeating this backward yields

\[ \overline K_1\in\mathcal C_{D-1}. \]

Finally \(K_1=UP_1U^\dagger\), and \(P_1\) was an arbitrary source logical Pauli. Hence

\[ \boxed{\overline U\in\mathcal C_D}. \]

The dimension enters only through the number of correctable regions. Each commutator consumes one region, so \(D\) spatial dimensions leave exactly \(D\) hierarchy levels.

7. Why the result also forbids a protected universal gate set

For a fixed number \(k\) of logical qubits, every finite level \(\mathcal C_j\) contains only finitely many elements up to global phase. This can be proved recursively. The Pauli group is finite up to phase. An element of \(\mathcal C_j\) is specified, up to phase, by its conjugation action on a finite generating set of Paulis, and every image lies in the finite set \(\mathcal C_{j-1}\). If two unitaries have the same conjugation action on every Pauli, their ratio commutes with the full matrix algebra and is a scalar.

There is one subtlety: \(\mathcal C_D\) is not generally a group when \(D\geq3\), so “every generator lies in \(\mathcal C_D\)” does not alone show that all generated products remain there.

The depth assumption closes this loophole. If a product of protected depth-\(h\) gates first left \(\mathcal C_D\) after a bounded number \(s\) of factors, that product would itself have depth \(sh=O(1)\). The theorem would put it back in \(\mathcal C_D\), a contradiction. Because \(\mathcal C_D\) is finite for fixed \(k\), the maximum possible shortest escape length over its subsets is finite. Thus, for all sufficiently large codes, the group generated by bounded-depth protected gates remains inside a finite set and cannot be computationally universal.

The fixed-\(k\) qualification belongs to this universality corollary. The main hierarchy bound itself is the local statement proved in Sections 4–6.

8. What is and is not an escape

Proposed maneuverDoes the theorem apply?Reason
Change the shape or tiling of a 2D stabilizer codeYes, if locality and macroscopic distance remainThe proof never uses a square lattice or translation symmetry
Use a geometrically local transversal physical gateYesIt is a particularly shallow local circuit; transversality alone does not make distant pairings geometrically local
Use a bounded-depth local unitary deformation between two codesYesThe morphism version allows distinct source and target codes
Braid defects through a chain of uniformly local 2D deformationsStill CliffordEach protected local step is Clifford, and the Clifford group is closed
Use lattice surgery with depth growing with code distanceNot as one constant-depth unitaryIts growing time depth lies outside the theorem's circuit assumption; its standard logical operations are nevertheless Clifford
Inject and distill magic statesInjection is outside the protected hypothesisAt injection, the new logical state does not yet have macroscopic topological protection
Use measurements and adaptive classical feedbackNot covered by the literal unitary-circuit statementA separate analysis is needed for nonunitary protocols
Couple distant planar qubits directlyNoThe gate range is no longer geometrically bounded in the planar metric
Let circuit depth grow until \(hr\) is comparable to \(d^{1/D}\)NoThe mesoscopic scale \(R\) in (4.1) no longer exists
Replace commuting Pauli checks by generalized non-Pauli stabilizersNoThe Pauli symplectic Cleaning and Union Lemmas used here no longer apply
Move from 2D to 3DYes, with a weaker ceilingThe allowed level rises from \(\mathcal C_2\) to \(\mathcal C_3\)

The theorem is a ceiling, not a construction. It says which hierarchy levels are impossible under its assumptions; it does not say that every gate below the ceiling is available for every code.

A current boundary case: “Clifford stabilizer codes”

Kobayashi, Zhu, and Hsin constructed a two-dimensional code with a transversal logical \(T\) gate in 2026 and explicitly described it as surpassing the Bravyi–König bound for Pauli codes. There is no contradiction. Their local “stabilizers” are Clifford unitaries rather than Pauli operators and need not commute. The object is therefore outside the abelian Pauli stabilizer formalism assumed in Sections 1–6.

This distinction is load-bearing, not terminological. The proof of the Cleaning Lemma used the symplectic vector space of Pauli operators; the Union Lemma factorized commutation with bounded-diameter Pauli generators; and equation (2.1) expanded errors in the Pauli basis relative to an abelian Pauli stabilizer codespace. Those steps cannot simply be reused for noncommuting Clifford generators. The 2026 construction changes the algebraic hypothesis that makes the Bravyi–König ceiling possible.

9. Audit questions

Q1. Why is the scale \(d^{1/D}\), rather than \(d\)?

A. A chunk of linear size \(R\) in \(D\) dimensions contains \(O(R^D)\) qubits. Distance guarantees correctability when that volume is smaller than \(d\), hence \(R\ll d^{1/D}\).

Q2. Where exactly is stabilizer locality used?

A. In the Union Lemma. Widely separated correctable chunks remain correctable because no bounded-diameter stabilizer generator can couple two chunks. Circuit locality is a separate use of geometry: it bounds light cones in the Causal-intersection Lemma.

Q3. Why may a non-Pauli commutator \(K_D\) be declared trivial in a correctable region?

A. Correctability plus Pauli expansion gives \(\Pi O\Pi=c(O)\Pi\) for every operator \(O\) in the region, not only for Paulis. Since \(K_D\) also preserves the codespace, its logical action is that scalar.

Q4. Why is the crossing-string argument insufficient as the general proof?

A. It presumes convenient string representatives. The theorem covers arbitrary local stabilizer codes. The simplicial partition and Cleaning Lemma manufacture the needed separated supports without assuming any particular shape for a logical operator.

Q5. Does the 3D conclusion provide a universal protected gate set?

A. No. It permits level-3 gates such as \(T\), but for fixed \(k\) the bounded-depth protected gates still generate only a finite subgroup inside the hierarchy ceiling. Additional unprotected or nonunitary resources remain necessary for universality.
